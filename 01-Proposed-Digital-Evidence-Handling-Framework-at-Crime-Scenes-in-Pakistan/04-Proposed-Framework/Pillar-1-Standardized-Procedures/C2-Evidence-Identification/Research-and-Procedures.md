# COMPONENT 02: DIGITAL EVIDENCE IDENTIFICATION, DEVICE CONDITION AND CLASSIFICATION

## Purpose

To identify potential digital evidence sources at the crime scene, assess their physical, operational and security conditions, and classify identified devices and digital evidence to support subsequent investigative activities.

## Main Activity 1: Identify Potential Digital Evidence Sources

### Step 1: Physical Search and Identification of Computers and Mobile Devices

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S01

**Purpose:**

To systematically locate all standalone computers, laptops, tablets, smartphones, and handheld digital devices across the crime scene.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Conduct a structured visual search pattern (grid, spiral, or zone search) across all authorized areas.
2. Inspect obvious locations (desks, tables) and concealed areas (drawers, under beds, closets, bags, vehicle compartments).
3. Identify desktop towers, server enclosures, laptops, tablets, smartphones, feature phones, and smartwatches.
4. Assign a unique physical exhibit identifier (EX-OI , EX-02) to each discovered device immediately upon discovery.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR.

**Required Documentation:** Physical Evidence Search and Identification Record (Form P2-01).

**Expected Output:** Identification and initial labeling of all primary computer and mobile hardware items.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 5.4.2 (p. 15), Clause 7.1.1.1 (p. 26).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 1 (pp. 1-7), Chapter 5 (pp 21-27).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix C (p. 19).
- council of Europe C-PROC (2019), section 3.3.2 (pp. 8-12).

### Step 2: Identification Of peripherals, Cables, power Supplies, and Removable Media

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S02

**Purpose:**

To locate all secondary storage media, power adapters, data cables, and peripheral hardware required for device operation and forensic reassembly..

**How to Perform It:**

1. Search for extemal hard drives, USB thumb drives, SD/microSD cards, optical discs (CD/DVD/Blu-ray), and magnetic tapes.
2. Search for power supply bricks, AC adapters, proprietary charging cables, and docking stations matching identified laptops/mobiles.
3. Search for extemal peripherals: mice, keyboards, webcams, hardware dongles, and security keys (e.g., YubiKeys).
4. Check optical drive trays and card reader slots on computers before moving them.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR.

**Required Documentation:** Physical Evidence Search and Identification Record (Form P2-01).

**Expected Output:** Complete collection Of supporting hardware, cables, and removable media required for forensic examination.

**Supporting References:**

- ISOIIEC 270372012, Clause 7.1.1.1 (pp. 26-27).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 1 (pp. 3-8), Chapter 5 (p. 31).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix C (p. 20).
- council Of Europe C-PROC (2019), section 3.3.2 (p. 10).

### Step 3: Identification Of Network Equipment and Infrastructure

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S03

**Purpose:**

To locate all networking hardware, broadband modems, wireless access points, network-attached storage (NAS), and network connection paths.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Inspect wall sockets, closets, and utility areas for routers, switches, network hubs, broadband modems (ADSL/Cable/F1ber ONT), and NAS units.
2. Trace network Ethernet cables from desktop PCs back to patch panels or router ports Identify Wi-Fi access points, mesh nodes, and cellular wireless modems.
3. Record SSID names, MAC addresses, and active status lights.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR.

**Required Documentation:** Physical Evidence Search and Identification Record (Form P2-01).

**Expected Output:** Mapping of all physical and wireless network entry points and storage infrastructure.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 7.2.1.1 (p. 36).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 1 (pp. 11-12).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix A (p. 15).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.3.2 (p. 10).

### Step 4: Identification of Specialized Devices (CCTV, IoT, Smart Home, Automotive)

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S04

**Purpose:** To identify non-standard embedded electronic systems, surveillance recorders, smart home sensors, and vehicle infotainment units containing potential evidence.

**How to Perform It**

1. Search for CCTV DVR/NVR units, digital video cameras, and standalone surveillance monitors.
2. Identify Internet of Things (IoT) devices: smart home hubs, voice assistants, smart sensors, security alarms, and smart meters.
3. Inspect vehicles at the scene for embedded Automotive IT (AIT) systems, dashcams, and GPS navigation units.
4. Note make, model, serial numbers, and storage type.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR / DES.

**Required Documentation:** Specialized and Embedded Device Register (Form P2-04).

**Expected Output:** Cataloging of non-traditional embedded digital evidence sources.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 7.3 (p. 40).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 1 (p. 9).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.3.2 (pp. 11–12).

### Step 5: Credentials and Access Information Gathering

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S05

**Purpose:** To lawfully locate, document, and secure paper notes, passwords, PIN codes, PUK numbers, and encryption passphrases required for device access.

**How to Perform It**

1. Search desks, drawers, monitor borders, keyboard undersides, and notebooks for handwritten passwords or PINs.
2. Search for original mobile phone packaging containing SIM card frames with default PIN/PUK codes printed.
3. Lawfully record statements from system administrators, device owners, or users regarding account credentials, BitLocker passphrases, or screen lock patterns.
4. Store captured credential notes in a secure, sealed evidence envelope separate from general view.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR / Investigation Officer.

**Required Documentation:** Passcode and Credential Seizure Log (Form P2-05).

**Expected Output:** Secure documentation of encryption passphrases and device lock codes.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 7.1.1.2 (p. 27).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 5 (p. 27).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix C, Section 15 (p. 19).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.3.2 (p. 10).

### Step 6: Evidence Dependency Mapping

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S06

**Purpose:** To document the physical and logical relationships between interconnected computers, external storage, peripherals, and network connections.

**How to Perform It**

1. Map which specific external hard drive, monitor, printer, and power supply connects to which desktop tower or laptop.
2. Map which IP camera connects to which specific channel on a CCTV DVR.
3. Assign matching sub-identifiers to dependent items (e.g., Tower EX-01; Power Cable EX-01-PWR; Ext Drive EX-01-HDD1).
4. Record the complete topology in an Evidence Dependency Diagram.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR / Scene Documentation Officer.

**Required Documentation:** Evidence Dependency Map (Form P2-06).

**Expected Output:** Complete architectural mapping enabling exact system reconstruction during lab examination.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 6.2.1 (p. 18), Clause 7.1.1.1 (p. 26).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 4 (p. 19), Chapter 6 (p. 35).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix C (p. 19).

### Step 7: Document Discovery Location and Contextual Relationship

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S07

**Purpose:** To record the exact physical placement and contextual setting of every discovered item without making unverified assumptions about user ownership.

**How to Perform It**

1. Record the precise physical location of each item (e.g., Room 2, East Wall Desk, top left drawer).
2. Document contextual observations (e.g., "laptop found powered on with user logged into account 'Admin'").
3. Avoid attributing device ownership to individuals based solely on proximity; record facts neutrally (e.g., "phone recovered from desk occupied by Subject X").

**Responsible Personnel:** Scene Documentation Officer.

**Required Documentation:** Device Context and Location Log (Form P2-07).

**Expected Output:** Objective, factual documentation of exhibit discovery context.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 6.6 (p. 21).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 4 (p. 19).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Section 4.3.1 (p. 8).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.3.1 (p. 7).

## MAIN ACTIVITY 2: DEVICE CONDITION ASSESSMENT

**Purpose:** To assess the observed physical, operational and security conditions of identified digital devices.

### Step 1: Device Power-State and Operational Assessment

**Procedure ID:** PI-C2-S01

**Purpose:** To determine whether a device is powered ON, powered OFF, in Sleep/Standby mode, or in an indeterminate state without altering its existing state.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Record whether the computer or peripheral device is powered ON or powered OFF.
2. Determine whether the device is in Sleep/Standby mode or an indeterminate state.
3. Assess the operational status of computer displays.
4. Assess the operational status of mobile communication devices.

### Step 2: Physical Condition Assessment

Assess the physical condition of identified devices, including:

- Undamaged.
- Cracked casing.
- Burnt connector.
- Liquid damage.
- Missing screws.

### Step 3: Security Condition Assessment

Identify:

- Encryption.
- Passwords.
- Other access restrictions.

### Step 4: Connectivity Status Assessment

Assess the connectivity status of identified devices, including:

- Ethernet cables.
- Wi-Fi connections.
- Bluetooth pairings.
- Cellular data connections.

### Step 5: Power Supply Condition Assessment

Determine whether the device is:

- Connected to an external power source.
- Operating on battery power.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR / Digital Forensic Unit (DFU).

**Required Documentation:**

- Device power-state and operational-status records.
- Records of physical condition.
- Records of available security and access restrictions.
- Connectivity status records.
- Power supply condition records.

**Expected Output:** A record of the assessed conditions of identified digital devices.

## Main Activity 3: Classify Digital Evidence

**Purpose:** To organize identified digital evidence according to its source, information type and volatility.

### 1. Classification by Device Type

Classify identified sources according to their device categories, such as:

- Computers and laptops.
- Mobile devices.
- Storage media.
- Network equipment.
- Specialized digital devices.

### 2. Classification by Evidence Type

Classify potential digital evidence according to the type of information, where supported by the investigation, such as:

- Documents.
- Images.
- Videos.
- Logs.
- Other digital information.

### 3. Classification by Volatility

Distinguish between:

- **Volatile Evidence:** Digital information that may be lost or changed when the device's operational state changes.
- **Non-Volatile Evidence:** Digital information retained in storage media.
