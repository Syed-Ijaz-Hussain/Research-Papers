# COMPONENT 01: CRIME-SCENE PREPARATION, AUTHORIZATION AND SECURITY

## 1. Objective

To ensure the crime scene is legally authorized, secured, and properly prepared, with competent personnel, appropriate resources, and necessary safety and evidence-preservation arrangements in place before digital evidence handling begins.

## 2. STEP-BY-STEP OPERATIONAL PROCEDURES

### Step 1: Verify Legal Authorization and Scope

**Procedure ID:** PI-C1-S01

**Purpose:**

To ensure investigators possess valid legal authorization before searching for, accessing or seizing digital evidence and understand the authorized scope and applicable restrictions.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Obtain and review the applicable search warrant, court order, statutory authorization or valid consent documentation.
2. Verify the specific physical addresses, digital storage locations, device categories, and temporal boundaries authorized for search and seizure.
3. Identify explicit legal restrictions, exclusions, or mandatory minimization requirements imposed by the issuing judicial authority
4. Brief the investigation team on the authorized search scope and applicable restrictions.
5. Record the relevant authorization details in the appropriate investigation record.
6. Ensure that any proposed search activity remains within the authorized scope.

**Responsible Personnel:** Crime Scene Supervisor.

**Required Documentation:** Legal Authorization and Scope Record.

**Expected Output:** Verified legal authorization and documented search boundaries and restrictions.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 6.7.5 (p. 22).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 3 (p. 15).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Section 3.3 (p. 7).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.2 (p. 6).

### Step 2: Brief Investigators and Establish Responsibilities, Competency and Escalation

**Procedure ID:** PI-C1-S02

**Purpose:**

To ensure that investigation personnel understand the investigation requirements, assigned responsibilities, competency limitations and procedures for obtaining specialist assistance.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Conduct a formal pre-operation briefing with all team members.
2. Explain the nature of the incident, investigation objectives and relevant legal restrictions.
3. Define individual roles: Lead Investigator, DEFR (First Responder), DES (Specialist), Documentation Officer, and Access Control Officer.
4. Explicitly instruct team members to disable Wi-Fi/Bluetooth on their personal mobile devices to prevent inadvertent RF interaction with scene electronics.
5. Explain procedures for avoiding unauthorized access, unnecessary interaction and alteration of potential digital evidence.
6. Decide which technical situations are too complex for ordinary investigators and when they must seek help from a qualified digital forensics specialist.

**Responsible Personnel:** Crime Scene Supervisor

**Required Documentation:** Investigator Briefing and Responsibility Assignment Record (proposed Form PI-02).

**Expected Output:** A briefed investigation team with clearly assigned responsibilities, understood competency limitations and established escalation arrangements.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clauses 6.7.1–6.7.3 (pp. 21–22).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Section 2.1 (p. 6).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.1 (p. 5).

### Step 3: Prepare Personnel and Required Resources

**Procedure ID:** PI-C1-S03

**Purpose:**

To ensure that the investigation team and required resources are prepared for the authorized crime-scene activities.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Confirm the availability of personnel required for the assigned investigation activities.
2. Verify the scene kit contains anti-static bags, Faraday isolation bags/boxes, write-blockers, sterile USB drives, camera gear, cable ties, tie-on exhibit tags, tamper-evident tape, screwdrivers, and paper bags.
3. Ensure all team members wear dean, dry, lint-free gloves.
4. Inspect Faraday bags for tears or shielding degradation.
5. Confirm that required equipment is ready for deployment.

**Responsible Personnel:** DEFR .

**Required Documentation:** Personnel and Equipment Preparation Checklist (proposed Form PI-03).

**Expected Output:** Prepared personnel and verified resources available for the planned crime-scene activities.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 6.9.3.1 (p. 24).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 2 (p. 13).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix C (p. 20).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.1 (p. 5).

### Step 4: Secure and Control the Crime Scene and Restrict Unauthorized Access

**Procedure ID:** PI-C1-S04

**Purpose:**

TO prevent physical or remote evidence tampering, accidental data alteration, unauthorized scene entry, or suspect interference with powered devices

**How to Perform It:**

1. Establish physical scene perimeter control immediately upon arrival.
2. Remove all unauthorized persons, suspects, and non-essential personnel from the immediate vicinity of digital equipment and power supplies
3. Place guards at the crime-scene entrances and exits to control the movement of people.
4. Require every individual entering or exiting the scene to sign the access log with full name, agency, role, entry time, exit time, and purpose
5. Prohibit any individual at the scene from touching, powering on, powering Off, or interacting with any computer, phone, or cable.

**Responsible Personnel:** First Responder

**Required Documentation:** Crime-Scene Access and Security Log (proposed Form PI-04).

**Expected Output:** A physically secured crime scene with restricted unauthorized access and documented scene-entry activities.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clause 6.2.1 (p. 18).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 3 (p. 15).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix C (p. 19).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.3.1 (p. 7).

### Step 5: Establish Crime-Scene Boundaries and Designated Evidence-Handling Zones

**Procedure ID:** PI-C1-S05

**Purpose:**

To partition the crime scene into functional operational areas, preventing cross-contamination and organizing physical evidence workflow.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Assess the scene geography and define Primary Evidence Zones (where digital hardware is discovered), Buffer Zones, and Command/Packaging Zones.
2. Establish a dedicated, clean evidence packaging area away from high traffic, direct sunlight, moisture, static generators, and strong electromagnetic fields (e.g., radio transmitters, large speakers).
3. Mark zone boundaries dearly using barrier tape or signag.

**Responsible Personnel:** Crime Scene Supervisor.

**Required Documentation:** Crime-Scene Boundary and Evidence-Zone Plan (proposed Form PI-05).

**Expected Output:** Defined and controlled crime-scene areas supporting organized evidence-handling activities.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clauses 6.2.1 (p. 18) and 6.9.3.2 (p. 25).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 3 (pp. 15–16).
- Council of Europe C-PROC (2019), Section 3.3.1 (pp. 7–8).

### Step 6: Assess Environmental and Physical Hazards

**Procedure ID:** PI-C1-S06

**Purpose:**

To identify environmental and physical hazards that may threaten personnel safety or affect the condition and preservation of potential digital evidence.

**How to Perform It:**

1. Inspect power cables, strip outlets, and UPS units for exposed wiring, electrical overload, or short-circuit risks.
2. Evaluate physical hazards, including structural instability, biohazards, fluid spills, or chemical contamination.
3. Check for physical anti-forensic traps (e.g., booby traps connected to power switches, magnetic destruction coils, or auto-wipe triggers).
4. Ensure investigators wear appropriate protective gear (lint-free gloves, anti-static wrist straps where required).

**Responsible Personnel:** Scene Safety Officer / DEFR.

**Required Documentation:** Scene Safety and Hazard Assessment Record (proposed Form PI-06).

**Expected Output:** Documented environmental and physical hazard assessment identifying conditions that may affect personnel safety and evidence handling.

**Supporting References:**

- ISO/IEC 27037:2012, Clauses 6.2.2 (p. 18) and 6.2.3 (p. 19).
- NIJ (2008), Chapter 3 (p. 15).
- ACPO v5 (2012), Appendix A (p. 18).
